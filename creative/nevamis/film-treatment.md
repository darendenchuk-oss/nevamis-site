# NEVAMIS — flagship film treatment

**Working title:** *You Still Decide*
**Length:** 58 seconds (inside the 45–75s target)
**Ratio:** 16:9 master. 9:16 and 1:1 derived with reframing, not cropping.
**Line:** The work moves. You still decide.

Checked against `truth-basis.md`. Nothing in this film shows a capability the
product does not have.

---

## The constraint that wrote the ending

NEVAMIS does not book into a calendar. Every tenant agent is provisioned with
`["end_call"]` and no booking tool. A person on the business's side confirms.

A weaker film would hide that. This one ends on it.

The last action is not the system completing a booking — it is **Ray, at his own
kitchen table, glancing at a phone and confirming with one tap**, then putting
the phone face-down and going back to dinner. One second of attention instead of
a lost evening. That is not a compromise forced by the truth; it is the truest
possible image of *the work moves, you still decide*, and it is the shot the
whole film is built to arrive at.

---

## Cast, such as it is

**RAY** — early 50s, electrical contractor, six vans. Competent. Grey at the
temples, hands that have worked. He is never flustered, never comic, never
rescued. He cannot answer the phone because **both hands are inside a panel**,
which is the honest reason and the sympathetic one.

**THE CALLER** — heard, not seen, until beat 7. A homeowner with a real problem
and no interest in whether Ray is busy.

NEVAMIS has no avatar, no face, no voice in the film. It is present as
**cool light and mono type**, and nothing else. The moment it acquires a
personality it becomes a robot, and the visual bible forbids robots.

## The ten beats

| # | ~sec | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–6 | **Both hands on the work.** Macro: a torque screwdriver seating a breaker. Warm worklamp, deep shadow, the hum of a panel. Ray's face lit from below by the lamp. No music yet — room tone, a ladder settling. |
| 2 | 6–11 | **The phone.** In his chest pocket, screen up, buzzing against the fabric. He does not look at it. He cannot. The buzz is the loudest thing in the mix. Hold one beat longer than is comfortable. |
| 3 | 11–17 | **It is answered.** Cut wide, cooler. Not to a screen — to the **van** on the driveway, dusk, one interior light coming up as the call connects. Mono type, small, lower-left: `CALL ANSWERED`. First mint of the film. |
| 4 | 17–24 | **It understands.** The caller's words appear as short mono fragments resolving into structured fields — *panel humming · no power to the kitchen · tonight if possible · Millwoods*. Not a HUD over a face. Type on black, composited with the real product's typography. Music enters: a low pulse. |
| 5 | 24–31 | **It checks the rules.** The rules are shown as **Ray's own words** — his handwriting from a shop whiteboard, dissolving into the mono list the system holds. `after 6pm → urgent only` · `no panel work quoted on the phone` · `Millwoods → yes`. This is the beat that makes it a platform and not a bot. |
| 6 | 31–37 | **It acts, inside them.** Job captured, address captured, **time window** captured — not a booking. `JOB CAPTURED` in mono. A quote request is *not* answered: the type shows the rule holding it back. Restraint here is the most persuasive second in the film. |
| 7 | 37–43 | **The customer is served.** First and only sight of the caller: a woman in her kitchen, phone to ear, shoulders dropping as she is told someone will confirm. Warm. She goes back to her evening. The business kept its promise without Ray touching it. |
| 8 | 43–48 | **The summary lands.** Ray, closing the van's rear doors. The phone lights his face — **warm practical and cool screen in the same frame**, the shot the visual bible reserves for exactly this moment. He reads it in two seconds. He does not stop walking. |
| 9 | 48–53 | **The same logic, wider.** The single flow we have watched repeats and multiplies — quietly, in mono, as a widening field: *leads · scheduling · follow-up · quotes · records · reporting*. Tiered honestly: the live one is solid, the rest are outlined. **Nothing here claims to be shipping.** |
| 10 | 53–58 | **You still decide.** Ray's kitchen. Dinner, other people, ordinary noise. The phone face-up on the table shows one line. He taps once. Turns it face-down. Someone passes him a dish. Cut to black on the sound of the room, not a sting. **NEVAMIS** — then the line. |

## What the film refuses to do

- **No robot, no brain, no orb, no HUD over a face.** The visual bible bans them
  and the ban is what makes beats 3–6 feel like software instead of sci-fi.
- **No fake UI text.** Every mono word on screen is composited from the real
  product's type in post. Generative video renders gibberish, and gibberish in a
  product shot is a lie about the product.
- **No calendar filling itself in.** See above. This is the one place a
  beautiful, conventional shot would make the film untrue.
- **No "never miss a call."** Blocked phrase, and the film does not need it.
- **No music swell over beat 10.** The emotional peak is a room full of ordinary
  noise. Everything before it earns the quiet.

---

## Sound

Physical first: panel hum, ladder, the buzz against fabric, van doors, a
kitchen. The system's presence is a **single confirmation cue** — a short,
low, two-note fall, more felt than heard. That cue is the NEVAMIS sonic motif
and it appears exactly three times: beat 3, beat 6, beat 10. Nowhere else.

Music: low pulse from beat 4, opening out through 8, and **out** before 10.

No whooshes. No riser into the logo.

---

## Why this film sells a platform

A viewer who watches only beats 1–8 sees a very good answering service.

Beat 5 is what changes it: the system is working from **rules the owner wrote**,
not from a script somebody sold him. Beat 9 widens that same logic. Beat 10
proves the owner never surrendered the decision.

That is an operating layer, shown rather than claimed — and it is all true
today, which is why it can be shown.
