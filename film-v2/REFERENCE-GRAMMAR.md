# REFERENCE-GRAMMAR

Measured from the supplied reference file, not inferred from description.
`Sentient-Desk-reference-clean.mp4` — **37.700s, 1280x720, 30fps**, sampled at
4fps (151 frames) and analysed for luminance, saturation and transition points.

**Grammar only. No artwork, geometry, wording, logo, colour or asset is copied.**

## What the measurement actually says

| Segment | Mean luminance (0-255) | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| 0-14s | **220.0** | A genuinely near-white field, not "light grey" |
| 14-20s | 50.7 | The transition zone |
| 20-34s | **8.9** | Near-black, far darker than a "dark navy" |
| 34-37.7s | **2.5** | The close resolves to essentially pure black |

**Exactly one major transition in the whole film: 15.25s, magnitude -218.2.**
Out of a 255 range. That is not a dissolve or a grade shift — it is a near-total
polarity inversion, and it is the film's single structural event.

## The finding that changes our work

| | Reference |
|---|---|
| Light movement saturation | **4.2%** |
| Dark movement saturation | **78.1%** |

**The light movement is almost entirely achromatic. Colour arrives *with* the
darkness.** That is the mechanism behind the restraint everyone perceives — the
first half withholds colour, so the inversion delivers two things at once
(darkness and saturation) rather than one.

**Our current A3 does the opposite.** Movement I places a strongly saturated
emerald/mint surface on the light field, which spends the colour before the
inversion can pay it out. The A3 direction is otherwise sound — its inversion
lands at 15.4-16.1s against the reference's 15.25s, which is close enough to be
the same instinct — but the colour discipline needs correcting.

**Recommended correction (not yet applied):** in Movement I, render the folded
signal surface at 10-18% saturation — a pale graphite-mint, almost a shadow —
and let full `#2FBF8F` / `#9FF0CE` appear only at and after the inversion. The
object stays the single primary visual; only its chroma is withheld. This costs
nothing and is a small edit to one `hue` value in `scene(t)`.

Second correction: our close currently sits on the same dark field as Movement
III. The reference drives its close **darker still** (2.5 vs 8.9) — the wordmark
lands in near-pure black. Worth matching; it is what makes the ending feel like
a resting state rather than another shot.

## Grammar we adopt

1. **Low information density, not short runtime.** 37.7s carrying very little.
2. **One primary object per frame.**
3. **Short centred fragments**, one at a time.
4. **One decisive inversion**, not several transitions.
5. **Colour withheld until the inversion.** *(newly measured)*
6. **Brand reveal near the midpoint**, at the hinge.
7. **Brief interface proof** — long enough to prove, not to explain.
8. **A close that is darker and quieter than everything before it.**

## What we deliberately do not take
Its artwork, its geometry, its wording, its logo, its palette, its motif shapes,
its music, or its runtime as a target for its own sake.
